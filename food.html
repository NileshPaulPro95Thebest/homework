Building a Healthy Plate

Why a Balanced Meal Matters?

A healthy plate gives our body the energy it needs to study, play, think, and grow. It should include colourful fruits, fresh vegetables, grains, and protein-rich food.

Learn more about healthy eating here: MyPlate Nutrition Guide

Smart Snack Choices

Snacks can be healthy when we choose foods that give energy instead of only sugar. Fruits, nuts, sprouts, and yoghurt are better choices than chips and sugary drinks.

Explore more nutrition facts here: WHO Healthy Diet Guide

Nutrition Tips for Students

Drink Enough Water

Water helps the body stay active, supports digestion, and keeps the brain focused. Students should carry a water bottle and drink water throughout the day.

Eat Colourful Food

Different colours in fruits and vegetables give different nutrients. A colourful plate often means a healthier plate.

Read more about healthy food choices here: Nutrition.gov

Quick Nutrition Note

Try this simple rule: fill half your plate with fruits and vegetables, one quarter with grains, and one quarter with protein.

Let's look about all the nutrients!!

Nutrients Features Sources
Carbohydrates The body's primary energy source Grains, Bread, Fruits, Vegetables
Proteins Built from amino acids, they repair cells, build muscle, and support immunity Meat, Fish, Eggs, Dairy, and Beans
Fats Store long-term energy, protect organs, and support cell membranes Nuts, Seeds, Oils, and Dairy
Vitamins Organic compounds (like Vitamins A, B-complex, C, D, E, and K) that support immunity, vision, and blood clotting • Vitamin A: Sweet potatoes, carrots, spinach, kale, pumpkin, and beef liver.
• B-Vitamins: Whole grains, meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, and dark leafy greens.
• Vitamin C: Oranges, strawberries, bell peppers, broccoli, kiwi, and Brussels sprouts.
• Vitamin D: Fatty fish, fortified milk/plant milk, egg yolks, and direct sunlight exposure.
• Vitamin E: Sunflower seeds, almonds, spinach, avocados, and wheat germ oil.
• Vitamin K: Kale, spinach, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, and green cabbage.
Calcium Builds strong bones and teeth while enabling muscle movement and nerve signaling. Milk, cheese, yogurt, fortified plant milks, tofu, and kale.
Iron Carries oxygen in the blood from your lungs to the rest of your body Red meat, lentils, spinach, pumpkin seeds, and fortified cereals.
Magnesium Regulates muscle contractions, controls blood sugar, and supports immune system function. Dark chocolate, almonds, pumpkin seeds, spinach, and black beans.
Pottasium Controls fluid balance inside cells and maintains a healthy, regular heartbeat. Bananas, sweet potatoes, avocados, spinach, and coconut water.
Sodium Maintains fluid balance outside cells and regulates blood pressure alongside potassium. Table salt, celery, beets, milk, and most processed or restaurant foods.
Phosphorus Works with calcium to build bones and manages energy storage in cells. Meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, nuts, and whole grains.
Iodine Creates thyroid hormones that control your metabolism, growth, and energy levels. Iodized salt, seaweed, cod, dairy products, and shrimp.
Zinc Powers wound healing, genetic cell production, and your immune defense system. Oysters, beef, chickpeas, pumpkin seeds, and cashews.
Selenium Acts as an antioxidant that protects cells from damage and keeps the thyroid working Brazil nuts, tuna, halibut, sardines, and shrimp.
Copper Helps make red blood cells and maintains healthy blood vessels and nerves. Liver, oysters, shiitake mushrooms, dark chocolate, and sunflower seeds.
